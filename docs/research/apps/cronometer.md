## Review themes

Sample: App Store listing reviews (US and CA, about 12 visible), Google Play summary via AppBrain, MyNetDiary February 2026 scorecard (64 reviews analysed), Trustpilot (12 reviews), Cronometer forum threads, Reddit roundup, editorial reviews. Shares are rough estimates across roughly 100 reviews and posts, not a statistical count.

| Theme | Praise or complaint | Approx. share | Example (paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accurate data and deep micronutrient tracking | Praise | ~35% | A user with a medical diet said the detailed vitamin and mineral breakdown changed how they manage their eating. |
| Generous free tier, better than MyFitnessPal | Praise | ~15% | Several reviewers switched from MyFitnessPal and said the free version already does more. |
| Intrusive ads in free version (full-screen, unskippable video, audio) | Complaint | ~15% | A forum user said ads that block the app mid-log push them toward uninstalling rather than paying. |
| Clinical, dated or overwhelming interface, steep learning curve | Complaint | ~12% | Reddit users describe it as powerful but cold and confusing for beginners (Reddit summary via a competitor blog, unverified). |
| Device sync and integrations (Apple Health, Garmin, Fitbit, Oura) | Praise, with some bug reports | ~8% | A user had a band stop syncing burned calories; support fixed it and they kept using it. |
| Bugs, slow web app, UI regressions, slow support | Complaint | ~8% | A Trustpilot reviewer got no reply to tickets and had to post on Reddit to get help. |
| Gold price and household cost, plus billing after cancellation | Complaint | ~5% | A reviewer and their partner liked the app but found paying for Gold twice too much for one home. |
| Weak food sharing between people in the same home | Complaint | ~2% of store reviews, but the most repeated request in forums | A Gold member said Cronometer beats MyFitnessPal in every way except sharing meals with a spouse. |

Partner / couple mentions: **8 distinct sources** found, all asking for the same thing. 7 Cronometer forum threads (2018 to 2025) plus 1 App Store review. What people ask for:
- Push or copy a shared meal from one diary into a partner's diary, optionally split by percentage (2018 thread, 12+ comments; staff said a family plan with easier food sharing "would be great") [17], [18].
- View a friend or spouse's day and copy it, as MyFitnessPal allowed (2020, revived 2022) [19].
- One person logging for both partners from one phone (2021) [21].
- Mutual diary visibility so the more consistent partner motivates the other (Aug 2025, both partners on paid Gold) [22].
- A household or shared subscription price (App Store review) [23].
None of these have shipped as of 2026-10-02. No request for shared workouts or shared streaks was found, likely because Cronometer is not used for lifting.

## Takeaways for FitPartner

- **Borrow:**
  - Trust positioning on data accuracy: verified food sources and a clear statement that AI never invents nutrient values. Worth copying in FitPartner's food data choices and copy.
  - Photo Log flow that always ends in a human review step, plus suggesting hidden items (cooking oil, sauces).
  - "Create recipe from selected diary items" as a fast way to reuse a shared meal.
  - Logging streak with a home screen widget and "streak to beat" framing.
- **Beat:**
  - The top unmet request from couples in Cronometer's own forum since 2018: log once, add to both diaries with a portion split. FitPartner should make "we ate the same dinner" a one-tap action.
  - Shared real-time view of both partners' calories and protein. Cronometer staff confirm accounts cannot see each other.
  - Household pricing. Two Gold subs cost CAD 159.98/year in Canada, and reviewers say that is too much for one home. One couples price under that is a clear wedge.
  - Strength logging. Cronometer logs exercise by time and calories, not sets and reps, so couples who lift need a second app.
  - Friendly, low-overwhelm UI. Cronometer's main complaint after ads is that it feels clinical.
- **Skip:**
  - 95-nutrient depth, nutrition scores and clinical reports. FitPartner's users need calories, protein and weight, not calcium absorption.
  - Ad-supported free tier with full-screen ads. It is Cronometer's biggest source of anger.
  - Professional (Pro) tier for dietitians.

**Workout tracking:** Yes, added via a separate app, MacroFactor Workouts (launched January 2026 on iOS and Android). Features: generated programs based on goals, level and equipment; auto-progression; 900+ exercises with technique videos; sets, reps, RIR, drop sets, partials, supersets, myoreps; plate calculator; rest timer; multiple gym profiles; PR and volume charts; Jeff Nippard program import; spreadsheet program import; Apple Watch app with Live Activities. The two apps sync body metrics, scale weight, progress photos, habits and period tracking, but nutrition coaching does not adjust from training data at launch. [2], [5], [9], [14], [19]

**Other notable features:** Apple Watch app for Nutrition (September 2025) with voice logging, weight logging and complications [19]; Wear OS listed only as "actively exploring" [14]; Expenditure Modifiers using step data (2025 to 2026) [14]; period and habit tracking [6]; customizable dashboard [20]; data export [5].

**Scale (company stated):** 600,000 users as of September 2026, up from 400,000 in September 2025 [14]. Workouts page states 200k+ downloads (company claim, not independently verified) [5]. No revenue figures published. Ratings: Nutrition 4.8 on US App Store (about 23K ratings) [6]; Google Play 4.7 with about 12.5K Android reviews (third-party estimate, marlvel.ai, June 2026, unverified) [21]; Workouts 4.8 on US App Store (about 4,600 ratings) [9].

## Review themes

Based on roughly 40 to 50 review snippets and summaries across the US and Canadian App Store listings, JustUseApp, two independent reviews, and third-party review aggregators. Shares are rough estimates of that sample, not a statistical count.

| Theme | Praise or complaint | Approx. share | Example (paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adaptive algorithm and science-based coaching | Praise | ~25% | Targets adjust every week from real weight data, so they feel trustworthy, unlike static calculators in MyFitnessPal. |
| Fast, clean logging and verified database | Praise | ~15% | Barcode scan, copy and paste, and the timeline food log make entry quick and accurate. |
| Non-judgmental tone and responsive developers | Praise | ~15% | The app never shames you for going over, and the team actually replies to feedback. |
| Workouts auto-progression and Nippard programs | Praise | ~10% | Smart progression and program import beat Hevy and Fitbod for serious lifters. |
| Price and no free tier (paywall right after signup) | Complaint | ~15% | Asking for payment after a one-week trial feels steep when free trackers exist. |
| Database gaps (regional, including Canadian products) | Complaint | ~7% | Many Canadian grocery items and regional SKUs are missing. |
| Learning curve and clinical feel, no social or gamification | Complaint | ~8% | Powerful but takes time to learn and offers nothing motivating beyond the numbers. |
| Workouts polish and progression jumps, no Wear OS | Complaint | ~5% | Recommended weights sometimes jump without explanation and the app still feels unfinished. |

Partner / couple mentions: 0 found in the review sample. No App Store, aggregator or review-roundup text mentioned a partner, spouse, couple or training with someone. Two independent reviews (Outlift, Macaron) list the absence of social or community features as a weakness, not as a user request. Reddit could not be searched directly (blocked), so partner requests there are unconfirmed. Indirect signal: the only sharing feature (recipes and custom foods by link) is explicitly framed by MacroFactor's help center as a way to send recipes to friends or family, which suggests household meal sharing is the main shared use case. Competitor forums (Cronometer) show recurring requests to log the same meal into a partner's diary, which MacroFactor also does not support.

## Takeaways for FitPartner

- **Borrow:**
  - Weekly adaptive targets based on trend weight rather than daily scale weight. Showing each partner a smoothed trend line avoids daily-weigh-in friction between partners.
  - Neutral, non-judgmental colours and language for going over targets. Reviewers repeatedly credit this; it matters even more when a partner can see your numbers.
  - Recipe share by link, extended into a shared household recipe and meal library both partners log from.
  - Multiple logging paths (search, barcode, label scan, describe by text or voice, photo) with an editable plate view before saving.
- **Beat:**
  - The whole social gap: MacroFactor has zero partner, friend or shared-progress features and nothing on its roadmap. A shared real-time dashboard plus a shared weekly workout streak is a clear point of difference against the most respected macro tracker.
  - One price for nutrition and lifting, and for two people. MacroFactor charges per app (USD $71.99/year each, or $89.99 bundle) per person, so a couple wanting both pays roughly USD $180/year. A couples plan undercuts this sharply.
  - "Log once for both": split a cooked meal between two diaries by portion. Not supported by MacroFactor and a known request in the category.
  - Integration between food and training: MacroFactor's two apps share body metrics but do not let training inform nutrition. FitPartner already has both in one PWA.
  - Web access: MacroFactor has no web app yet; a PWA covers phone and desktop from day one.
- **Skip:**
  - Competing on algorithm depth or a research-grade verified food database. That is MacroFactor's moat and needs a research team.
  - A hard paywall with no free tier. It is the most common complaint, and a partner product needs the second person to join with zero friction.
  - Native watch apps and AI program generation for now. High cost, and not what makes FitPartner different.

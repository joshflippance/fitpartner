## Review themes

Sample: 21 written reviews visible on the US (10), Canada (6), UK (2) and Australia (3) App Store pages, dated 2026-01 to 2026-09, in Apple's default ordering. Apple's RSS review feed was blocked (robots.txt), and no Reddit, Product Hunt or press coverage was found, so this is a small sample. Shares are rough counts within those 21 and overlap. Note the gap: the star average is 4.9 from 13K ratings, while the written sample holds many 1 star paywall reviews, so the written sample is far more negative than the ratings overall.

Partner / couple mentions: essentially every positive review is about a couple (husband, girlfriend, partner, long distance, busy professionals). What people ask for: a free or monthly option, logging without a photo (manual entry, location, steps), and fewer bugs. No review in the sample asks for nutrition, weight or lifting detail; buyers seem to choose it precisely because it has no stats. One reviewer values seeing missed workouts to spot patterns, which hints at appetite for simple history.

## Takeaways for FitPartner

- **Borrow:**
  - The core loop that 13K ratings validate: each partner sets a weekly target, check-ins appear instantly for the other, and the week resolves as a pair. FitPartner's "week counts when both hit their own target" is the same idea and is clearly marketable.
  - Low effort relationship mechanics with high charm: nudges, Pinky Promise (partner approves a forgotten workout), a jointly chosen reward when the streak goal is hit, and a partner widget on the home screen. Pinky Promise is a neat answer to the "forgot to log" problem for a shared streak.
  - Playful copy and weekly or monthly recaps that are shareable. Their release notes read like a couple joking with each other.
  - Optional photo on a workout as an extra, not a requirement.
- **Beat:**
  - Depth. Sweatmates deliberately tracks nothing beyond "worked out today". FitPartner shows calories, protein, weight and lifts on one shared dashboard, so couples who want results, not just attendance, have nowhere else to go in this niche.
  - Honest pricing and onboarding. Its biggest complaint is a hard paywall shown only after setup and partner invite, plus yearly only pricing and billing gripes. A free tier that lets two people pair and log, a clear price up front, and one subscription covering both partners would directly answer its 1 star reviews.
  - Platform reach. iOS only (Android unconfirmed); a PWA works for mixed iPhone and Android couples.
  - Flexible logging. Photo only logging annoys some users; FitPartner logs real workout data and can make a photo optional.
- **Skip:**
  - Lock In Mode (locking a partner's apps). Fun headline, but controlling, needs native Screen Time access a PWA cannot get, and the developer itself jokes it is slightly toxic.
  - Mandatory dual camera photos as the only proof of a workout.
  - AI generated art and AI video ads; reviewers call it deceptive.
  - Hiding the paywall until after onboarding.

## Threat assessment

**High on positioning, medium on product overlap.** Sweatmates owns the "couples fitness accountability" story on iOS with a strong scale signal (13K US ratings at 4.9, about 100K downloads by a third party estimate) and ships features roughly every two weeks, many of them aimed at the shared week (nudges, Pinky Promise, rewards, custom wagers). Any couple searching for a partner workout app on iPhone will likely find it first. It does not compete on what FitPartner tracks: no nutrition, no weight, no sets or loads, by explicit design, and no review asks for them. The real risk is that Sweatmates adds a simple calorie or weight check-in; the opening is couples who want results tracking plus the shared streak, on any phone, without a hard paywall.
